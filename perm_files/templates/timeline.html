Execution Timeline

Every recorded job on an absolute time axis. Bars are real start and end times, so overlap is real parallelism — not layout.

How to read this — click to collapse
grey prefix
Time the job sat queued after becoming eligible. A long grey bar means the scheduler, not your script, is what made it slow.
amber prefix
Queued more than 2s — starved. One job stuck at the head of the priority queue starves everything behind it, and this is how you see it.
coloured bar
Actual execution. Length is real duration and horizontal position is real start time, so two bars that overlap genuinely ran at the same moment.
amber outline
On the critical path.
  • The single longest chain of dependent jobs, by wall time.
  • Its length is the floor on how fast the run can be.
  • Everything off it has slack, so speeding those up changes nothing.
  • Share near 100% of wall clock: dependency-bound, more workers will not help.
  • Share well under 100%: capacity-bound, more workers will help.
parallelism
Total execution time of every job divided by wall clock.
  • 1.0 means nothing overlapped, strictly one job at a time.
  • 4.0 means four jobs ran at once on average.
  • Compare it against total worker slots: far below means capacity sat idle.
#2, #3
Retry attempt. Each attempt is its own span, so a job that failed twice before succeeding shows three bars.
PERM / EPH
Whether the worker that ran it is a permanent node or an ephemeral one the scaler spawned and may reclaim.
hover a row
Queue wait, execution time, worker, failure reason, and parents.
how far back
The in-memory ring holds up to 5000 spans and is cleared when the Master restarts. The from disk window options read persisted history instead, which survives restarts (retained 7 days by default, configurable).
